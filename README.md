## Conclusion

Garden earns less than Harbour because it gets about 68% of Harbour's traffic and because its recorded unit costs are 43% higher and its prices 5% lower on the same 45 products. Of the $281.54 daily gross profit gap, $145.58 comes from transaction count, $106.80 from cost and $33.59 from price. When we checked the cost and price gaps, we found they are exact formulas. Garden cost is Harbour cost times 1.43 and Garden price is Harbour price divided by 1.05, to the cent on every product, so they are settings and not supplier or recipe differences. Our recommendation is two actions that hold whether or not this file matches the real tills. First, run a four week 5% price test on one Garden category. It is worth up to $6,000 to $8,000 over July to December if no customers leave, and it breaks even if 9.6% leave. Second, staff and prepare for Tuesday at about 1.5 times the demand of other weekdays, since the spike appears in every branch, hour and product and no promotion, event or double posting explains it. We keep what we measured separate from what we estimated. The Tuesday spike and the exact formulas are measured, but real Stories shops show no Tuesday peak and the daily variation is as clean as pure chance, so the file may be generated or transformed. Because of that, Garden's cost gap is worth anywhere from $0 to $26,000, depending on whether the 1.43 is an accounting allocation or a real cash cost, and customer response to price was never observed. Checking a few raw Tuesdays against the till and asking finance what the 1.43 contains would settle most of this at no cost.
